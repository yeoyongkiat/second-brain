Very glad to have taken this short trip to Bangkok right after CNY to attend the Prince Mahidol Award conference over the weekend to share Singapore’s insight to our regulations and governance approach to mitigating risks of AI in healthcare. It was an interesting and insightful session, considering that understanding and adoption across multiple countries are still very varied. We concluded the following:

1️⃣ Critical to have regulatory clarity and good governance because this will allow stakeholders to forge forward with innovation, and health professionals to deploy and use the technology safely, ethically, responsibly and fairly in their practice. This will also engender public trust in the healthcare system.

2️⃣ However, considering the technology is still evolving quickly, we may not want to take a very hard stance yet on a full set of regulation or else innovation may be unduly stifled. This is why we launched the AI in Healthcare Guidelines in 2021 (and updating them in 2025) to improve understanding and codify good practices to complement existing regulations for AI developers, implementers and users.

3️⃣ The existing regulations focuses primarily on AI device safety and efficacy, but we need to move beyond device regulations to guide how licensed institutions should have the governance to adopt appropriate AI devices and how patient data is secured, as well as internal audit mechanisms and informed consent to allow safe use of AI by healthcare professionals for clinical practices. Hence, our regulatory guidances need to be holistic to cover device, service, data and professional related requirements.

4️⃣ Regulators may not be aware of the latest AI technologies, so we need to closely engage and consult the industry, institutions and professionals on the development and review of our regulatory guidances, so as to make the guidances more robust and outcomes based. We also cannot do this in isolation, so we need to partner with other global partners such as WHO, International Medical Device Regulators Forum and HealthAI - The Global Agency for Responsible AI in Health to learn best practices, benchmark regulatory approaches and share lessons learnt and early warning signals for registered devices.

5️⃣ Lastly, we mustn’t forget the users of who these AI devices are developed for - patients and healthcare professionals. So we need to properly educate them on the informed consent process , how AI is being used and understand the possible limitations and biases. Many AI devices can even be directly rolled out to consumers, so they need to know the risks for interpretation of the results and be aware of how their data is being stored.

Welcome your views and input on how you think regulations and governance of AI in healthcare should be better developed from your perspective of industry, healthcare institution, professional or a lay person.

Ricardo Baptista Leite, M.D. Peiling Yap Sameer Pujari Health Sciences Authority
