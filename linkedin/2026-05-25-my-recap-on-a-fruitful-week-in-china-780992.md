🇨🇳 🇸🇬My Recap On A Fruitful Week in China to Advance Regulatory Partnerships - let me have your views! Health Sciences Authority

This past week in China has been both intense and immensely rewarding—a powerful reminder that effective regulation extends far beyond borders.
Our shared mission is simple: to ensure that safe, effective, and innovative health products reach patients faster.

1. Strengthening Collaboration with China’s National Medical Products Administration (NMPA)

In Beijing, I had the privilege of congratulating and meeting the new Commissioner Huang and signed a renewed Memorandum of Understanding between HSA and NMPA.

This expanded partnership builds on over 2 decades of close cooperation and broadens our collaboration into emerging areas such as cell, tissue and gene therapy products. It also opens new opportunities to explore facilitative regulatory pathways, pilot innovative schemes, staff exchanges, and joint capacity building. Together, we lay the foundation for faster patient access to cutting-edge therapies in both countries.

2. Visiting Industry in Suzhou

I met and interacted with some promising SG and China biotech companies keen to set up in Biosparc, a lifescience park in Suzhou Industrial Park.

It was inspiring to see and hear first-hand from the companies regarding their scientific breakthroughs and how they translate into real-world healthcare solutions.

3. Convening Global Regulators at DIA 2026

The opening session of DRUG INFORMATION ASSOCIATION (DIA) amazingly brought together a cross-continental panel of regulators from US FDA, UK MHRA, Saudi FDA, ANVISA Brazil, Indonesia BPOM, China NMPA, Hong Kong DoH, and HSA.

Our discussion focused on how regulatory systems evolve to remain scientifically rigorous while becoming more agile, efficient, and innovation-friendly. It was encouraging to see consensus across to build trusted partnerships and collaborate on regulatory sciences to address increasingly complex healthcare innovations.

4. Engaging Industry Thru SG Town Halls

Throughout DIA, we met with numerous industry leaders and innovators.

Our message was clear: Singapore is committed to being a trusted and agile regulatory hub where companies can launch their products first—or simultaneously with major markets through our mutual reliance or work sharing schemes such as ACCESS or Orbis. In so doing, we create an environment where promising therapies and technologies can reach patients sooner.

5. Deepening Bilateral Relationships

We also had productive bilateral discussions with senior leaders from multiple agencies, from US FDA, NMPA, National Institute of Metrology and Tsinghua University to explore even closer collaboration, so as to advance regulatory excellence and shape the future of global healthcare!
