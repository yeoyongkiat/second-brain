Honoured to represent Singapore at the AHPRA (Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency)’s National Scheme Combined Meeting 2025, where I delivered the keynote address on: “Transforming Regulation in the Age of Direct-to-Consumer Services: How Do We Balance Innovation, Access and Safety?”

In today’s rapidly evolving healthcare landscape, regulators are at a crossroads — navigating technological advancements, new care models, and rising public expectations. I was proud to share how Singapore is actively transforming regulation to meet these challenges head-on, including using my digital twin to showcase what a future AI consultation may be like (thanks to Joachim for helping with this!)

At the heart of my message was the REACH framework, a guiding approach we use to ensure regulations are responsible, future-ready, and people-centred:
✅Risk-based decision-making
✅Empowerment of stakeholders and consumers
✅Agility in regulatory approaches
✅Collaboration across global and local ecosystems
✅Holistic and continuous regulatory review

A great highlight was hearing Tony Lawler from Australia’s Therapeutic Goods Administration reminding us that: “Good regulation is like underpants — invisible when it works well, but essential, and definitely noticed when it doesn’t!” 😝😝 Indeed a perfect reminder of the quiet, often unappreciated but critical role we regulators play. Thanks to Chris Robertson for the invite and facilitating our keynote session too!

Later, I joined a plenary with Nirvana Luckraj and Nick Lord to explore:
“Can Transactional Healthcare Still Be Patient-Centred?” My answer: Absolutely. With the smart use of AI and digital tools, we can move beyond one-off encounters to continuous care — freeing up clinicians to focus on what matters most: building trusted and more relational relationships with patients.

It was an inspiring and collaborative meeting. One where regulators AHPRA, Therapeutic Goods Administration and Health Sciences Authority across borders came together with a shared purpose — to protect patients, empower professionals, and shape a healthier future.

Let’s continue and co-create the next chapter of healthcare regulation together — one that is agile, accountable and anchored in safe patient care, while promoting innovation in care models and technologies and advancing professional competencies!

Thanks to APHRA for the invite and opportunity to network and exchange with the various professional boards and members!
